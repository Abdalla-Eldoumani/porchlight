How it works

A light left on for every neighbour.

Porchlight helps nurses, home-care workers, family and volunteer neighbours keep an eye on people who live alone, and gets someone to the door when they don't answer, especially when the cold or the power grid turns dangerous.

Why Calgary

On a normal day, living alone is fine. On the coldest night of the year, with the grid one step from rolling blackouts, it means nobody might notice if the heat goes off, a phone dies, or someone falls.

177,405Calgarians aged 65 or older in 2021, up 28% in five years.
22%of people 65 and older in the Calgary area lived alone in 2021.
150+volunteer-run community associations already know their streets.

The City of Calgary lists living alone, being 75 or older, low income and trouble getting around as risk factors for isolation. Those are the people Porchlight is for.

The night it nearly happened

Saturday, January 13, 2024. It was −31 °C at Calgary airport by 7 PM and Alberta's grid ran short of supply. The demo replays that evening from the official records.

5:32 PMFor six minutes, every bit of that backup is in use.
6:44 PMEvery phone in Alberta gets an emergency alert asking people to cut their electricity use.
9:00 PMSupply is back to normal.

How it works

  1. A small box by the door.I'm OK, Help, and a light that says what's going on. No camera, no microphone, nothing to wear.
  2. It watches the weather and the grid.When it's −25 °C or colder and the grid operator is on alert, every box asks: are you OK? One press answers.
  3. No answer in 30 minutes, or a Help press?Porchlight asks the first volunteer on that person's list, a neighbour they already know, in the volunteer's own language, on their phone.
  4. Nobody takes it?After 15 minutes (5 for a Help press) it moves to the next volunteer, then to the community coordinator. Someone always owns the call.
  5. The volunteer taps "I'm going", then "I'm with them, all good".The box tells the person who is coming and roughly when, so they're not left wondering.
  6. A caregiver can ask any time."Ask for a check-in" on the dashboard makes the box buzz and ask. No I'm OK within 5 minutes, and the card says so, so the caregiver can send someone.

It also notices a missed daily check-in and a box that has gone quiet. Every rule is written down and tested; the AI only writes the wording of messages, it never decides who to ask.

Privacy, safety, and who's in charge

What we keep, and what we don't

  • Button presses, a plain "someone moved" signal if the box has a motion sensor, the times, and which volunteer helped. That's it.
  • The AI that writes volunteer messages sees a first name, the community, what happened and the weather. Never an address or a phone number.
  • No camera, no microphone, no location tracking.
  • Coordinators sign in; volunteers get a private link that only shows the people they're asked about.

What Porchlight is not

  • It is not 911. Every Help message tells the volunteer to call 911 if it looks like an emergency.
  • It doesn't guess about health. Messages say only what happened.
  • If the box loses power or internet, that alone asks a volunteer to check.

Made for the people who'll use it

For the person at home

  • Nothing to log into, nothing to charge, nothing to wear.
  • The light shows a picture and a word, never colour alone.
  • It works the same at 3 AM as at noon.

For volunteers and coordinators

  • Messages in English, Arabic, Chinese and French.
  • Bigger text and high contrast buttons at the top of every screen.
  • Every status pairs a symbol with a word, and everything works from the keyboard.

What it costs to run

A community association could start this on a bake-sale budget.

One box, as we built itabout US$59 for the board, plus buttons and a case
One server for a whole communityabout US$5 a month
Who runs itthe community association that already knows the street, with its own volunteer list

A cheaper box for a real rollout only needs Wi‑Fi, a button and a light; the server doesn't change.

Built with

Arduino UNO Q for the box. Python and FastAPI on the server. Tiger Data (TimescaleDB) for the history of presses and grid readings. Gemini writes the volunteer messages. Auth0 signs coordinators in. Hosted on Vultr.
